\# ROGUE ABYSS — ART BIBLE



\## Visual identity



> This document prevents Rogue Abyss from drifting into generic Roblox fantasy, bright children's-game aesthetics, or asset-store visual clutter.



\---



\# 1. ART DIRECTION NORTH STAR



Rogue Abyss should look like:



> \*\*A mysterious fantasy game from the mid-1980s, remembered through the imagination of someone who found an old game box in an attic and rebuilt the world in modern 3D.\*\*



The visual direction takes \*\*high-level inspiration from the mood of Rogue (1985)'s cover art\*\*:



\* dramatic fantasy

\* strong silhouettes

\* darkness

\* mysterious environments

\* theatrical composition

\* old illustrated-book feeling

\* danger without excessive gore

\* warm light against deep shadows



Do NOT copy the original Rogue cover.



Do not recreate:



\* its exact composition

\* characters

\* typography

\* poses

\* objects

\* artwork

\* logos



Use the era and mood as inspiration.



\---



\# 2. THE MOST IMPORTANT VISUAL RULE



\## Do not make Rogue Abyss look like generic Roblox.



Avoid:



\* glossy plastic

\* neon outlines

\* rainbow colors

\* giant glowing objects

\* oversized cartoon faces

\* generic fantasy armor

\* excessive bloom

\* huge floating UI

\* exaggerated rarity colors

\* toy-like environments

\* "YouTube thumbnail" composition



The game should feel authored and restrained.



\---



\# 3. VISUAL PERSONALITY



Rogue Abyss should feel:



\* ancient

\* quiet

\* mysterious

\* dangerous

\* slightly surreal

\* tactile

\* lonely

\* intelligent

\* handcrafted



It should NOT feel:



\* childish

\* hyperactive

\* shiny

\* noisy

\* cute by default

\* corporate

\* generic



\---



\# 4. COLOR PALETTE



Use a restrained palette.



Primary colors:



\* charcoal

\* soot

\* aged stone

\* dark brown

\* old iron

\* muted brick

\* parchment

\* desaturated green

\* muted rust

\* deep burgundy



Lighting accents can use:



\* warm amber

\* pale candlelight

\* muted gold

\* cold moonlight

\* occasional desaturated blue



Bright colors should be rare.



A rare color should communicate something.



\---



\# 5. CONTRAST



Do not make everything high contrast.



Contrast should communicate importance.



High contrast can indicate:



\* danger

\* discovery

\* a landmark

\* an important object

\* a major event



Normal rooms should have controlled contrast.



If everything is visually loud, nothing is important.



\---



\# 6. DARKNESS



Dark does not mean pitch black.



The player must still be able to understand the environment.



Use subtle differences between:



\* wall

\* floor

\* doorway

\* object

\* creature

\* shadow



The dungeon should be mysterious without becoming unreadable.



\---



\# 7. LIGHTING



Preferred lighting sources:



\* candles

\* lanterns

\* braziers

\* cracks of daylight

\* strange environmental glow

\* reflected light

\* distant rooms



Lighting should guide the player's eye.



Not every room should be equally illuminated.



Some darkness should be intentional.



\---



\# 8. MATERIALS



Materials should feel:



\* rough

\* old

\* matte

\* worn

\* physical

\* imperfect



Preferred:



\* stone

\* wood

\* iron

\* aged cloth

\* old masonry

\* dusty surfaces



Avoid:



\* shiny plastic

\* chrome fantasy armor

\* excessive metallic reflections

\* perfectly clean materials



\---



\# 9. GEOMETRY



Use stylized but substantial Roblox geometry.



The environment should have:



\* strong arches

\* chunky masonry

\* readable stairs

\* deliberate door frames

\* interesting floor patterns

\* occasional architectural irregularities



Do not make procedural rooms look like:



> "a bunch of square Parts connected together."



\---



\# 10. ROOM COMPOSITION



Every important room should work as a still image.



Freeze the game.



Imagine taking a screenshot.



Ask:



\* Is there a focal point?

\* Is the lighting intentional?

\* Is the silhouette readable?

\* Does the room tell me something?

\* Is there visual depth?

\* Is there negative space?

\* Does the room feel different from the previous one?



If the answer is no, redesign the room.



\---



\# 11. ARCHITECTURAL LANGUAGE



Use several visual architectural families.



\## THE OLD STONE



Heavy masonry.



Arches.



Worn floors.



Ancient construction.



\---



\## THE IMPOSSIBLE REPAIR



Walls have been repeatedly modified.



Different materials meet.



Old architecture has been patched with strange solutions.



The player should wonder:



> "Who kept repairing this place?"



\---



\## THE BURIED MACHINE



Mechanical structures exist underneath the fantasy architecture.



Old mechanisms.



Chains.



Gears.



Sealed doors.



Unclear purpose.



\---



\## THE FORGOTTEN SHRINE



Sparse.



Symmetrical.



Deliberate.



Almost too clean.



These rooms should feel different from normal dungeon spaces.



\---



\## THE LIVING PASSAGE



The environment appears to be changing.



Organic textures.



Unusual growth.



Subtle movement.



Architecture that doesn't seem completely inert.



\---



\# 12. ENVIRONMENTAL STORYTELLING



Tell stories without paragraphs of text.



Use:



\* abandoned supplies

\* worn paths

\* repaired walls

\* damaged doors

\* repeated symbols

\* strange footprints

\* blocked passages

\* discarded objects

\* rooms repurposed over time



The player should discover history naturally.



\---



\# 13. PLAYER CHARACTER



The player character should be visually simple.



From the gameplay camera they should have:



\* recognizable silhouette

\* limited color palette

\* clear facing direction

\* readable movement



Do not make the player brighter than the entire environment.



\---



\# 14. CREATURES



Creatures should not look like default Roblox NPCs.



Avoid:



\* default humanoid bodies

\* giant cartoon eyes

\* goofy expressions

\* excessive saturation

\* generic fantasy monsters



Prefer:



\* strange silhouettes

\* memorable shapes

\* unusual movement

\* restrained colors

\* readable behaviors



A creature should be recognizable from its silhouette before its details.



\---



\# 15. CREATURE DESIGN RULE



Visual design should communicate behavior.



For example:



A creature that listens might have:



\* unusual head orientation

\* exaggerated ear/sensory shapes

\* slow deliberate movement



A creature that patrols might have:



\* clear directional posture

\* consistent movement animation



The player should eventually associate appearance with behavior.



\---



\# 16. ITEMS



Items should look physical.



Avoid floating glowing pickups unless there is a strong reason.



An item should look like something the player could actually pick up.



Examples:



\* old keys

\* bottles

\* tools

\* strange relics

\* cloth bundles

\* mechanical components

\* hand-written scraps

\* carved objects



Items should be visually distinctive without using giant floating rarity beams.



\---



\# 17. VFX



VFX should be restrained.



Good:



\* dust

\* smoke

\* sparks

\* embers

\* subtle magical anomalies

\* mist

\* environmental particles



Bad:



\* giant explosions every few seconds

\* neon shockwaves

\* screen-filling particles

\* constant glowing outlines



VFX should feel like punctuation.



\---



\# 18. ANIMATION



Movement should have weight.



Prefer:



\* deliberate movement

\* subtle idle behavior

\* readable attacks

\* physical reactions

\* slow environmental motion



Avoid:



\* exaggerated bouncing

\* constant dancing

\* hyperactive animation

\* unnecessary screen movement



\---



\# 19. UI VISUAL LANGUAGE



The UI should feel like part of the same mysterious artifact.



Use:



\* thin borders

\* restrained typography

\* subtle textures

\* dark surfaces

\* parchment-like accents

\* simple icons



Avoid:



\* giant rounded mobile-game buttons

\* rainbow rarity colors

\* glowing neon frames

\* oversized badges

\* "LEVEL UP!!!"

\* constant reward animations



\---



\# 20. TYPOGRAPHY



Typography should feel slightly historical without becoming difficult to read.



Use a distinctive display typeface only for:



\* title

\* major chapter/floor names

\* important menus



Gameplay information must remain highly readable.



Never use decorative typography for critical instructions.



\---



\# 21. AUDIO VISUALIZATION



Audio should influence the visual language.



A quiet room can be visually sparse.



A dangerous room might use:



\* deeper shadows

\* subtle environmental movement

\* distant light

\* stronger silhouettes



Do not simply add red lighting whenever something is dangerous.



\---



\# 22. ROBLOX THUMBNAIL



The thumbnail should NOT contain:



\* shocked faces

\* "FREE!"

\* giant damage numbers

\* ten characters fighting

\* rainbow loot

\* giant glowing swords

\* generic fantasy warriors



Instead:



One strong composition.



Potential structure:



\* mysterious dungeon opening

\* lone explorer

\* powerful silhouette

\* warm light

\* darkness surrounding the subject

\* one strange object or doorway



It should look like:



> \*\*a lost fantasy game box from another timeline\*\*



rather than a typical Roblox thumbnail.



\---



\# 23. ART QUALITY GATE



A feature fails visual review if it looks like:



\* generic Roblox

\* mobile idle game

\* children's YouTube content

\* generic fantasy RPG

\* asset-store collage

\* AI fantasy wallpaper

\* neon battleground



A feature passes when it feels:



\* authored

\* restrained

\* atmospheric

\* strange

\* tactile

\* coherent

\* memorable



\---



\# 24. FINAL VISUAL RULE



When deciding between:



\*\*more effects\*\*



and



\*\*better composition\*\*



choose better composition.



When deciding between:



\*\*more detail\*\*



and



\*\*stronger silhouette\*\*



choose stronger silhouette.



When deciding between:



\*\*more color\*\*



and



\*\*more meaningful color\*\*



choose meaningful color.



Rogue Abyss should look confident enough to leave empty space.
